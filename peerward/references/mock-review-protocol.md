# Mock Peer-Review Protocol

Use this reference to generate a professional mock review, train a student to review, or perform an editor-style pre-submission stress test.

## Confidentiality and authorization gate

Before processing non-public review material:

- confirm the user is authorized to provide it;
- keep the material in an approved environment;
- do not upload confidential manuscripts to public issues, examples, or repositories;
- do not claim that a tool is private unless its retention and reuse controls have been verified.

If journal or institutional policy prohibits AI use in peer review, do not process the manuscript. A mock review for the author is not an official journal review and must be labeled accordingly.

## Reviewer stance

A useful review is:

- evidence-based, courteous, specific, and constructive;
- focused on the manuscript, not the author's motives or identity;
- explicit about whether a judgment follows from data, manuscript text, field knowledge, or reviewer opinion;
- calibrated to the journal and article type;
- designed to help the editor decide and the author improve.

Do not:

- invent literature or claim exhaustive novelty checking without a documented search;
- demand citations for reviewer self-promotion;
- use ad hominem language;
- reject an alternative theory merely because it differs from the reviewer's preference;
- treat language quality as a substitute for evaluating scientific validity;
- present the recommendation as the editor's final decision.

## Review sequence

### 1. Scope and evidence declaration

State:

- material reviewed;
- unavailable files or evidence;
- target venue and article type, if known;
- whether literature verification was performed;
- confidence and important assessment limits.

### 2. Independent manuscript summary

Summarize the problem, method, principal claim, and evidence without copying the abstract. If the manuscript cannot be summarized coherently, record that as an architecture risk.

### 3. Overall assessment

Discuss:

- significance and audience;
- novelty relative to the stated baseline;
- formulation and assumptions;
- method clarity and implementability;
- theoretical validity;
- experiment design, data analysis, reproducibility, and limitations;
- whether conclusions are supported by the design and evidence.

### 4. Major comments

Each major comment must contain:

1. manuscript location or evidence;
2. precise deficiency;
3. scientific or editorial consequence;
4. required outcome, not a prescribed pet solution;
5. examples of sufficient evidence or revision;
6. severity and confidence.

A major comment concerns validity, novelty, interpretation, missing evidence, reproducibility, or a manuscript-level argument failure.

### 5. Minor comments

Use minor comments for bounded clarification, notation, local presentation, reference accuracy, figure readability, or language issues that do not determine the central conclusion.

### 6. Recommendation

Use one of:

- ready after minor bounded changes;
- substantial revision needed;
- fundamental redesign or new evidence needed;
- outside assessed scope.

Explain the reasoning and unresolved blockers. Do not predict acceptance probability.

## Professional report template

- **Mock-review label and evidence boundary**
- **Summary**
- **Strengths**
- **Major comments**
- **Minor comments**
- **Questions requiring author verification**
- **Recommendation and confidence**
- **Confidentiality reminder**

## Quality control

Before returning the review, check:

- every major comment is supported by manuscript evidence;
- requests are feasible and proportionate;
- comments distinguish missing explanation from missing science;
- no comment speculates about author intent;
- no confidential content is reproduced beyond what is necessary;
- recommendation follows from the listed concerns;
- journal-specific instructions override this general protocol.
