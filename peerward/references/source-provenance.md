# Source provenance and copyright boundary

Peerward's writing guidance is an original synthesis. It does not reproduce textbook chapters, scanned files, exercise sets, or extended passages.

## Locally consulted sources

- Jerzy Trzeciak, *Writing Mathematical Papers in English*.
- Steven G. Krantz, *A Primer of Mathematical Writing*.
- Donald E. Knuth, Tracy Larrabee, and Paul M. Roberts, *Mathematical Writing*.
- Hilary Glasman-Deal, *Science Research Writing: For Native and Non-Native Speakers of English*.
- Graham C. Goodwin and Stefan F. Graebe, *A Doctorate and Beyond*.
- Peter J. Feibelman, *A PhD Is Not Enough! A Guide to Survival in Science*, second edition.
- John N. Tsitsiklis, *A Few Tips on Writing Papers with Mathematical Content*.
- João P. Hespanha, *Writing a Control Paper*.
- Philip E. Bourne and collaborators, the *Ten Simple Rules* collection.
- Kwan Choi, guidance on publishing in leading journals.

A locally supplied Chinese compilation, *科技论文写作技巧大汇总*, was used only as a historical navigation source for topics such as manuscript revision, referee perspective, and author responses. Because it aggregates materials of mixed and sometimes unclear provenance, it is not treated as an authoritative source and no text, figures, or internal examples are reproduced.

One supplied DJVU source was not indexed because a compatible local parser was unavailable; no claims in this release depend on it.

## Use policy

- Paraphrase principles in original language.
- Use synthetic control, optimization, and multi-agent examples.
- Cite source titles when provenance matters.
- Do not upload local source files or reveal their filesystem locations.
- Do not include enough sequential detail to substitute for a copyrighted book.
- Treat venue-specific author instructions as controlling when they conflict with general advice.
- Treat current publisher and journal policies on AI use and confidential peer review as controlling; do not infer that a general-purpose tool is approved for official review work.
