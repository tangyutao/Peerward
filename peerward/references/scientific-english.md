# Scientific English for mathematical papers

Apply this reference after scientific logic and section placement are sound.

## Information flow before grammar

For each sentence, identify old information, the new message, and its connection to the previous sentence. Put familiar context before the main new result when doing so reduces ambiguity. Keep one dominant logical action per sentence.

## Paragraph audit

A paragraph should have:

- a controlling purpose visible near the beginning;
- sentences that develop one line of reasoning;
- explicit contrast, cause, consequence, or limitation when the relation is not obvious;
- an ending that delivers the paragraph's scientific point or leads to the next move.

Split paragraphs that perform unrelated literature review, contribution, and implementation tasks.

## Sentence audit

- Prefer direct subjects and active verbs when the actor or method matters.
- Break sentences that contain nested causal order or repeated “since/which/whereas” structures.
- Make pronoun references unambiguous; replace vague “this” with the actual result, assumption, or procedure.
- Preserve parallel grammar for parallel conditions or contributions.
- Remove redundant openings, but retain words such as “that” or “then” when they make parsing safer.
- Avoid subjective difficulty labels such as “trivial” and “obvious.”

## Mathematical language

- Write quantifiers in words in prose when symbols would interrupt reading.
- Do not begin a sentence with a mathematical symbol.
- Treat displayed equations as grammatical parts of sentences.
- Use “an” rather than “the” for solutions or equilibria unless uniqueness is established.
- Distinguish approximation, implication, equivalence, necessity, and sufficiency precisely.

## Language-resource boundary

Lists of common phrases can help multilingual authors notice conventional moves, but Peerward must not assemble prose by copying stock sentences. Generate wording from the paper's actual logic and verify that conventional language does not conceal an unsupported claim.
