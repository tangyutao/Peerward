# Section playbooks

Use only the section relevant to the task.

## Title

- Name the searchable problem and result class.
- Match the strongest theorem; do not advertise a broader graph, plant, uncertainty, or optimality class.
- Remove decorative phrasing and unsupported words such as “novel.”

## Abstract

Build five compact moves:

1. precise problem and setting;
2. obstacle or missing capability;
3. method or mechanism;
4. principal guarantee/result;
5. evidence, implication, or limitation when important.

Keep it self-contained. Avoid citations, displayed equations, unexplained abbreviations, and claims not established in the paper.

## Introduction

Use a flexible progression:

1. establish the specific field/problem and significance;
2. synthesize the most relevant existing approaches;
3. locate the technical or evidential gap;
4. state the paper's answer and contributions;
5. explain scope and, when useful, organization.

Every contribution should answer a gap already made visible. Compare with closest work by technical dimensions, not citation count.

## Problem formulation

State in a recoverable order:

1. entities and dynamics;
2. inputs, disturbances, outputs, and constraints;
3. information and communication access;
4. assumptions and solution concept;
5. objective and success criterion;
6. implementability boundary.

For distributed methods, distinguish the global mathematical objective from local data and neighbor-only computations.

## Main results and algorithm

- State the main result early and cleanly.
- Explain module roles and signal interfaces before detailed equations.
- Put gain and parameter selection rules where an implementer can find them.
- Separate inherited component results from the new closed-loop or composition result.

## Experiments

Assign each experiment one evidential role:

- mechanism demonstration;
- robustness or sensitivity test;
- fair baseline comparison;
- ablation;
- scaling behavior;
- practical interpretation.

Match baselines on problem, objective, constraints, and information. With randomness, report repeated runs and uncertainty. Discuss failure regions when they define the method's boundary.

## Conclusion

Reinforce the result's meaning rather than repeating the abstract. State limitations and future work at the level of concrete assumptions, regimes, or open questions. Do not introduce new evidence or inflate the claim.
